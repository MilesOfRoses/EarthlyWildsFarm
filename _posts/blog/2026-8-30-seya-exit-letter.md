<b><h1 id="essay">An essay</h1></b>

<p>
I was encouraged to share my thoughts in a leaving letter because, as someone
no longer living at Twin Oaks, I'm less precariously positioned to point out issues. I
question the value of articulating the issues since those perpetuating them will
dismiss this out of hand and say nasty things about me, and those with whom this
resonates might well continue to stay silent out of fear. Hopefully preaching to
the choir will at least give the ones who need it a sense of catharsis; however, I
would urge you all to start speaking out and commit to supporting each other and
standing up for each other publicly, each and every time they target one of you.
</p>

<p>
<b><i>Racism</i></b>
<br>
Twin Oaks is dominated by white fragility--and fragility in general around any
issue that requires privileged parties to engage in self-reflection or admit to
having made mistakes/held erroneous beliefs.
I can only imagine how it feels to be a person whose skin color doesn't give them
the option of easily sliding under the radar if desired; to be lied about, bullied,
and put through endless process over nothingburgers, while white members who
rape and steal (supposedly some of the actions most antithetical to policy and
norms) are treated with patience; to speak out in favor of Twin Oaks following its
purported values around "eliminating the effects of racism" and face even more
sanctioning than that incurred by white people saying the same things. Even as a
white ally, I received social shunning, hyperbolic and nasty communications, and
character assassination, which I was expected to absorb gracefully. My only
regret about my time at Twin Oaks, other than upholding a particular CMT
decision during my plannership, is that I tried to be the bigger person instead of
matching energy.
I believe there are members of color here who will say I don't have the right to
speak for them. You're absolutely correct. I'm not speaking for y'all. I'm speaking
for those your community treated like garbage until they left, those who had the
same messaging as I did but were punished the more for it due to their lack of
white privilege. They matter too, although you don't seem to think so. And I'm
speaking for myself, a person who doesn't like to witness stunning levels of
injustice and hypocrisy from a place I'd hoped to call home.
</p>


<p>
<b><i>Ableism</i></b>
<br>
Toward the end of 2025, following a more flagrant instance of anti-autistic
ableism against a prospective new member, there began to be hopeful movement
forward on this issue. This was met with regressive backlash similar to that
around land reparations occurring at the same time, a backlash that has persisted
for many months and now encompasses most domains of life at Twin Oaks. There
were disingenuous insinuations made that progress toward equitable treatment of
neurodivergent folks "directly resulted" in the squatter situation we dealt with for
the first few months of 2026 (see also "Authoritarian Turn and Corruption").
Twin Oaks seems to resent the disabled, from those with chronic illness/injury, to
neurodiverse people, to those experiencing a mental health crisis (see also "A
Sociocultural Clone of Mainstream Capitalism"). Ethical problems with this
valuation and treatment of human beings aside, this will have ramifications for
the community's survival if left unchecked.
Humans have succeeded as a species through group selection. Individuals did not
survive by being the most bootstrapped independent silos, nor communities by
being monolithic; communities survived through the wide range of skills and
experience that a variety of individuals brought to the group. If you don't want
"weird" people in your community, or "people who get taken off the labor
system", or people whose mental health struggles appear only as a liability to
you--you're going to lose a lot of the variation in skillsets, knowledge, aptitudes,
life experience, and companionship that is essential for a community to remain
viable.
</p>



<p>
<b><i>Classism</i></b>
<br>
Twin Oaks struggles to move past its upper-middle-class white roots. This
presents additional hurdles for anyone who hasn't kept much company with, or
had a similar education as, this demographic. Classism dovetails with other -isms
including those already discussed. Those who can't or won't culturally assimilate
to the community's well-to-do whiteness often experience more problems finding
labor (a process which is absolutely a social activity and something of a hazing
ritual for new members) and issues with having their voices heard. If a comment
isn't written or spoken in the "correct" way, its content is disregarded (see also
"Exceptionalism").
The most impactful issue of class at Twin Oaks is differential access to outside 
resources, both financial and social. Having family or friends that can finance
travel and deposit steady streams of money into gift accounts allows for an upper
tier of members who have significantly better quality of life. This of course
means there is a lower tier of members who experience a good deal more chronic
stress, from which there is little relief and whose effects can be cumulative.
The upper tier get more creature comforts, morale boosters, self-actualization
experiences such as travel/retreats/concerts, and time away from the
community to de-stress and re-center when they need it. I'm not arguing that
they shouldn't get these things. I'm pointing out that having a life of greater ease,
and then turning around and putting less fortunate members under a microscope
for hiccups in their money or labor balance, resembles mainstream attitudes such
as "Have you tried just not being poor anymore?" and "I've got mine, screw you"
(see also "A Sociocultural Clone of Mainstream Capitalism").

</p>



<p>
<b><i>A Sociocultural Clone of Mainstream Capitalism</i></b>
<br>
The community functions more like capitalism than egalitarianism, with humans
being valued based on how much work they do and whether they do the "right"
work (the term for this is "workism"). Whether a prospective new member already
has an extensive resume seems more important than their work ethic, love of
learning, creativity, kindness, compassion, innovation, or other positive qualities
that make community thrive. Those who struggle to find work, to work through
illness or disability, or who suggest that quota could be lowered/eliminated are
viewed with dislike and suspicion.
Since Twin Oaks offers few cultural, philosophical, and ethical divergences from
the capitalist mainstream, the community seems to feel a subconscious need to
distinguish itself somehow, most notably by the visible and smell-able lack of
cleanliness characterized as ruggedness, simplicity, or environmentalism. A lot of
cultural value and social approval is placed on not purchasing items or not doing
work that would impart cleanliness.
</p>



<p>
<b><i>Hierarchy</i></b>
<br>
Aside from informal socioeconomic tiers (see "Classism"), there is an
unacknowledged hierarchy in play around types of membership. Visitors and
guests provide indispensable labor that no one else wants to do, do it
uncomplainingly since it impacts their ability to stay, and are often treated as
nuisances. New members do much of this labor as well and spend months 
carefully monitoring their behavior, then when they're full members they are
treated as though their opinions don't matter. Only longer-term members seem
entitled to have opinions or a voice in what happens at Twin Oaks, some showing
little gratitude for the revolving door of folks who contribute so much.
An interesting twist is that it's not the longest-term members who are most
implicated in perpetuating this hierarchy. In fact, many of them have also been
left feeling silenced, targeted, and unappreciated this year by a cadre of
medium-long-term members. This faction of members in the 2 to 10 year range
appear to center themselves at the top of the hierarchy; they seem to see
themselves as having more of a right to speak than new members, yet claim that
longer-term members don't deserve special treatment for their tenure.
</p>





<p>
<b><i>Exceptionalism</i></b>
<br>
Some expect rules and norms to apply to others but not to themselves, especially
around communication. The most shocking and cruel things can be said and
written, if done in the appropriate "egalitarian" and "policy-compliant" way, if
one has the right group of friends to join them in going on the offensive while
also defending them.
</p>






<p>
<b><i>Factionalism, Pettiness, and Grudges</i></b>
<br>
A remarkable amount of labor hours--in a community that is stingy with hours in
most situations--have recently been poured into process around years-old
grudges. Factions form and strengthen around attempts to kick out guests or take
peoples' managerships. Think of how nice the community could be if all that
energy and all those hours were put toward something positive instead of sowing
division and resentment. Someone from another community recently told me that
this kind of behavior simply would not be tolerated in their community, and that
those engaging in it would be shown the door.
</p>




<p>
<b><i>Authoritarian Turn and Corruption</i></b>
<br>
The confluence of misfortunes (Harmony fire, squatter, mental health incidents,
internal conflict) at the start of the year has been leveraged to prop up the
conservative backlash against the hopeful social progress that was occurring at
Twin Oaks in the latter half of 2025. The squatter, a once in 60-year occurrence,
was used as pretext to impose stricter guesting policy that is having negative
impacts on members. Surveillance and monitoring of labor and money budgets,
targeting of individuals for personal and illegitimate reasons, corruption and
overreach by persons on CMT/PTM/Econ Team and others, is not--or rather,
should not be--normal. I admit to being perplexed that many don't seem to see
how closely this all parallels the current presidential administration.
</p>



<p>
<b><i>Cultural Hegemony and Dissent</i></b>
<br>
Those who speak out about these problems have been treated badly and vilified
both during and after their membership. Even the gentlest of suggestions that the
community has work to do in order to follow the values stated in its bylaws gets
met with hostility and punishing/silencing measures, and then the same parties
carrying out these measures wonder why progressive voices in the community
become less polite and more strident.
</p>



<p>
<b><i>Cultlike Tendencies</i></b>
<br>
"Against policy" and "inegalitarian"--If you were to replace these with "condemned
in the scriptures" and "sinful", the way these terms are regularly leveraged to
delineate an ingroup, attempt behavior modification, and exert
control/chastisement would be quite similar.
I found cult deprogramming literature helpful and illuminating in my journey to
leaving Twin Oaks. If you're feeling like there's something weird going on and can't
quite put your finger on it, if you're feeling shamed or gaslit or ostracized for
having what seems to you like a pretty normal reaction to unhealthy things you
witness and experience here, look into this. An institution doesn't need to be
religious or have a charismatic leader to have cultlike, abusive traits. You deserve
better. Good luck!
</p>
